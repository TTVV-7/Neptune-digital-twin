# Construction equipment in the library

The library (**L**) now has 19 construction machines, all at real scale, in four groups: **Heavy equipment**,
two new categories, **Cranes & lifts** and **Material handling**, and more trucks under **Vehicles**.
Every one of them can be driven: select it (or stand within 25 m of it) and press **V**.

## What was added

Sizes are length × width × height in metres, as shown on the library cards. Top speeds are in km/h
(**Shift** adds a third, as for the other vehicles). All models are from Sketchfab under
[CC Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); `public/library/CREDITS.md` credits them.

## Things to know

- **Two 20 t excavators.** The built-in *Excavator (20 t)* is the one that digs. *Excavator, detailed (20 t)*
  is a realistic model that only drives; use it where looks matter more than digging.
- **The cranes are set up for work**, not folded for travel: the all-terrain crane's boom is raised to 16 m and
  the crawler crane's lattice boom reaches 45 m. Their card sizes include the boom, so place them by their
  carriers and tracks, not by the box. The crawler crane creeps at 2 km/h, as real ones do.
- **The telehandler's stabilisers are drawn lowered**, so it is 3.6 m wide here; it travels at about 2.6 m.
- **The boom lift is a 40 ft machine.** No clean 60 ft model was found.
- **The container handler is a made-up design** ("a fictional container loader"): a fixed 8.3 m spreader and a
  9 m mast, where a real one telescopes from 6 to 12 m and stands 11 to 13 m. Fine as a stand-in at a glance.
- **Brand marks** show on some models: CAT (excavator, grader), JCB (backhoe), Genie (both lifts), SkyTrak in
  large letters along the telehandler's boom, Toyota on the forklift's mast, a Volvo grille badge (dump truck),
  and model lettering on the two rollers (BW 145 D-3, ARP 35). The licences cover the models, not the
  trademarks; avoid these models in material that could look like an endorsement.
- **Looks:** the dozer is battered and rusty; the two rollers are photo scans with the lighting baked in; the
  doty_aecom models (wheel loader and the three trucks) are clean flat-colour models in the same style as the
  jobsite trailer and tool crib, and the dump truck and mixer have European and 1950s-style cabs.
- **Provenance:** the doty_aecom models carry SketchUp's default material names. That is what any model made in
  SketchUp looks like, so it isn't evidence of copying from 3D Warehouse (whose terms forbid redistribution),
  but the origin can't be confirmed. The jobsite trailer and tool crib already in the library share this.

## Site trailers, temporary covers, water treatment and people (built in)

23 more items are generated in code rather than downloaded (`src/trailers.js`, `src/covers.js`,
`src/water-treatment.js`, `src/people.js`, all listed in `src/procedural.js`): boxy site equipment
whose size matters more than its surface detail. They're drawn at their real size, with no files to
download and no licence to credit, and each one's parts are merged into one mesh per material
(`src/parts.js`), so even a fabric shelter's trusses are a handful of draw calls. Thumbnails are
rendered in the app like the other built-in pieces. Sizes are as on the library cards, length ×
width × height in metres, measured over everything (stairs, hoses, braces).

### Tests

`npm run test:site-items-ui` builds each of these and checks it's within 5% of its real size,
stands on the ground and is a handful of meshes; that it's listed in its category with its size and
a thumbnail; and that it can be placed (one with the mouse), turned with **R**, saved and reloaded.
Together they add no model files (0 MB) and about 12 KB of compressed code; building all 23 takes
about 60 ms.

## For maintainers: how they were imported

Each model went through `npm run add-asset` with its real length (or a scale where the posed length differs
from the real one, and always for the rigged telehandler). A few needed fixing first with gltf-transform:

- The two cranes came from one file holding both; each was split out, and the cab interiors (textured with
  Unreal's placeholder grid) were removed.
- The backhoe, telehandler and forklift drew their whole bodies with see-through (BLEND) materials, which
  three.js draws without depth, so far sides show through near ones; they were made opaque. The backhoe and skid steer also lost glass transmission, which
  costs a second render of the whole scene.
- The grader's hydraulic hoses (88k triangles) were dropped, and the dozer's 441 small meshes were joined so they
  could be simplified.
- The dump truck, skid steer and both cranes had their node transforms baked into their vertices. three.js sizes
  a model from each mesh's box turned by its node, and for turned parts that box is loose: the dump truck sat a
  metre above the ground and the cranes were off-centre. If a future import floats or sits off-centre, this is
  the likely cause; it would be worth baking transforms in `add-asset` or measuring exact boxes in `models.js`.
- The telehandler is rigged, so its card size was corrected by hand to the pose it is drawn in.
- The thumbnails of the cranes (Sketchfab's showed both) and the dump truck (a distant top view) were rendered
  in the app instead.
- Each machine's `drive` entry (top speed, acceleration, braking, reverse speed in `library.json`) was added by
  hand. Running `add-asset` again for an id (to try another `--front`, say) keeps it, and for the same model
  also keeps the thumbnail (`--new-thumb` fetches Sketchfab's again), an earlier `--credit` and a rigged model's
  hand-corrected size, rescaled to the new scale and front.

In total the 19 models add 25.6 MB of GLB files (the largest is the dozer at 2.9 MB) and 78 KB of thumbnails.
