          Drive anywhere on Earth in 3D

          World Drive is a free arcade driving game that runs in your browser, with nothing to download. Pick any place on the globe, from your own street to Times Square or a pass in the Alps, drop in and drive it in photorealistic 3D: the 3D world is Google's map of the real one, so the buildings, bridges, hills and roads are where they really are.

          What you can do

          • Drive anywhere. Search for a city, a landmark or an address, type coordinates, or tap the globe.
          • Stunts. Hit a wall and you bounce off it into the air: spin, flip and roll, land on your wheels to score, and chain tricks into combos.
          • Boost and drift. The booster takes you into overdrive, a drift pays out a mini-turbo, and glowing pads on the road (switched on in the pause menu) fill the booster up.
          • Fly. Turn your ride into a plane and fly over the city or a crater, roll and loop it for points, then bail out, free-fall and parachute back down.
          • Drive on the Moon. Switch the globe to the Moon and drive its craters, seas and the Apollo landing sites, in low gravity, with the Earth in the sky.
          • Drive on Mars. Switch the globe to Mars and drive Olympus Mons, the canyons of Valles Marineris and where the rovers landed, in 38% of the Earth's gravity, under a dusty butterscotch sky.
          • Drive on Vesta. Switch the globe to the asteroid Vesta and drive the giant Rheasilvia basin and its central peak, the Snowman's craters and the troughs round its middle, in 2.5% of the Earth's gravity, so a hop lasts about six times as long.
          • Drive on Ceres. Switch the globe to the dwarf planet Ceres and drive Occator and its bright salt spots, the lone ice volcano Ahuna Mons and the great basin Kerwan, in 2.9% of the Earth's gravity.
          • Discoveries. On the Moon, Mars, Vesta and Ceres, drive to real named places, from the Apollo and rover landing sites to Olympus Mons and Rheasilvia's central peak, to collect each one's card of real facts and its height in the mission data the ground is built from, and fill each world's collection book. Press L to colour the ground by its real measured height.
          • Drive with others. See who's driving nearby as ghost cars, on every world, or invite friends to a party with a link, wherever each of you is. More on driving with friends.
          • Levels and the leaderboard. Driving earns XP for 50 levels of cars, paint, boost trails and titles, and your best runs go on the leaderboard.
          • Challenges. A weekly and a monthly challenge on each world, the Earth, the Moon, Mars, Vesta and Ceres: race through checkpoints, collect stars or drive to a landmark against the clock, each with its own board of the best times.
          • Your ride. A pickup, a go-kart or a muscle car to start, and six more cars as you level up, from a golf cart and a tuk-tuk to a formula racer, all painted in the Garage; the optional Supporter Pass adds more.

          How to play

          W S gas and brake, A D steer, Space drift (tap to jump), Shift boost, F plane (Q E roll it, Z X loop it, F again to bail out), R back on the road, L the ground's real heights on another world and M back to the globe (on the globe, M shows the worlds). On a phone or a tablet, buttons on the screen.

          Questions

          Is it free?
          Yes. The 3D map costs World Drive a little for every visit, so everyone gets one free Earth drive a day (and there's a limit for everyone together); after that the page opens on the Moon, and the Moon, Mars, Vesta and Ceres are free and unlimited. The Supporter Pass (bought once and kept, or for 24 hours) adds rides and looks and more Earth drives a day; you never need it to play.
          Does it work on a phone?
          Yes, in the browser on a phone, a tablet or a computer with 3D graphics (WebGL). Touch screens get driving buttons.
          Can I drive my own street?
          Yes: search for your address. Where Google's 3D map has no buildings modelled, you drive on the land itself.
          Can I drive on other planets?
          Yes: the Moon, Mars and the asteroids Vesta and Ceres, each built from NASA's maps of its real ground, in its own low gravity, free with no daily limit. See driving in space.
          Can I drive on the Moon?
          Yes: press Worlds at the top of this panel and pick the Moon, or pick one of its places above. Its mountains, craters and seas are the real Moon's, from NASA's maps, and its gravity is a sixth of the Earth's, so a jump lasts two and a half times as long. You can drive with others there too: those near you on the Moon, and your party's friends wherever they are.
          Can I drive on Mars?
          Yes: press Worlds at the top of this panel and pick Mars, or pick one of its places above. Its volcanoes, canyons and craters are the real Mars's, from NASA's maps, and its gravity is 38% of the Earth's, so a jump lasts about 1.6 times as long. You can drive with others there too, in Mars's own rooms: those near you on Mars, and your party's friends wherever they are.
          Can I drive on Vesta?
          Yes: press Worlds at the top of this panel and pick Vesta, or pick one of its places above. Vesta is an asteroid 525 km across, far from round, and its basins, troughs and craters are the real Vesta's, from NASA's Dawn mission: Rheasilvia's central peak rises about 20 km over its basin floor. Its gravity is 2.5% of the Earth's, so a hop stays up about 6 times as long. You can drive with others on Vesta too, in its own rooms.
          Can I drive on Ceres?
          Yes: press Worlds at the top of this panel and pick Ceres, or pick one of its places above. Ceres, a dwarf planet and the biggest body in the asteroid belt, is the real Ceres from NASA's Dawn mission: Occator crater and its bright salt spots, and Ahuna Mons, a 4 km ice volcano. Its gravity is 2.9% of the Earth's, so a hop stays up about 6 times as long. You can drive with others on Ceres too, in its own rooms.
          Can I play with friends?
          Yes. Invite friends gives you a link; whoever opens it joins your party and can drive beside you, on any world. Cars are ghosts that pass through each other, and there's no chat. See driving with friends.
          What does World Drive keep about me?
          Only what the leaderboard and levels need, and never where you really are: see About World Drive.

          World Drive

          Pick a spot anywhere on Earth, drop in and drive the real world in 3D: city streets, mountain passes, bridges, wherever you like. Or switch to the Moon, Mars, Vesta or Ceres and drive that.

          Made by Thomas in Vancouver, Canada. Follow along on Instagram: @printyours.ca.

          The 3D world is Google's photorealistic map, streamed as you go. Every drive on it costs a little, so everyone gets one free Earth drive a day, with a limit for everyone together; the globe you pick a place on is our own picture of the Earth (NASA's Blue Marble), and the 3D map is only opened when you press Drive here on the Earth. Past either limit, the Moon, Mars, Vesta and Ceres are still free and unlimited. An Earth drive lasts as long as you keep the page open: fly off and drop in somewhere else as often as you like.

          The leaderboard keeps the nickname you pick and your best scores, with where you set them and in what, your best time on each challenge you finish (with when you passed each gate and in what), and your XP and level, with the areas and start spots you've driven (until you pick a nickname, that's kept in this browser); nothing else about you.

          While Show me to drivers nearby is on (it is unless you switch it off in the pause menu), or while you're in a party, the others driving there see your leaderboard nickname (or a guest name) with your level and title, your ride and where your car is in the game, including the spot you dropped in and its name. Nothing else about you, and never where you really are.

          With it on, you're also counted on the globe's map of who's driving now, which everyone sees: a dot, to about a kilometre, where your car is in the game (or, while nobody else is driving in your area, where you dropped in, which can be a place you searched for), with how many are driving there. No name, no place name and nothing else about you. In a party, on the globe, or with it off, you're not counted.

          To see how the game is played, World Drive counts, for each day, things like how long people drive, on the Earth, the Moon, Mars, Vesta or Ceres and in which rides, which start spots they drop in at and how long that took, roughly where else they drop in (to about a kilometre, counted once a day for each visitor at each place), how far into the game a visit gets, whether it's on a phone, a tablet or a computer, how many searches are made (never what was typed), what they change in the Garage, what went wrong (a place with no 3D map, a ride that didn't load), which site they came from and how long the page took to load. Only totals are kept, with nothing about who: no cookies, and nothing kept that tells one visitor from another (to count a visitor once a day at a place, a keyed hash of their address and the place is kept that day and then deleted; Cloudflare's backups of the game's storage can hold it for up to 30 days more, and only someone with the game's own secret key could check an address against it).

          Signing in, with Google or with a username and password, is only for the Supporter Pass and to keep your level and times on every device. With Google it keeps your Google account's name and email address; with a username, the username and a salted hash of your password (never the password itself), and no email. Either way it keeps whether you have the pass and which nickname is yours, so the pass and its ⭐ follow you to any browser. Your username is only for signing in: the boards show your nickname. Payments are made with Stripe: World Drive never sees your card.

          The Moon: its pictures and heights are NASA's, from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter's cameras (LROC) and laser altimeter (LOLA), as NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio's CGI Moon Kit has them; the small craters and the rocks close by are made up. On the Moon it's the same as on the Earth: with Show me to drivers nearby on, or in a party, the others driving there see your nickname, level and title, your ride and where your car is on the Moon, and you're counted on the Moon's map of who's driving now; a party's friends on the Earth see that you're on the Moon, and where.

          Mars: its heights are NASA's, from Mars Global Surveyor's laser altimeter (MOLA), as NASA's Planetary Data System has them, and its colours the Viking orbiters' photographs of all of Mars, as the USGS's colour mosaic has them; the small craters, the rocks close by and Phobos's place in the sky are made up.

          Vesta and Ceres: their heights and pictures are from NASA's Dawn mission (NASA/JPL-Caltech/UCLA/MPS/DLR/IDA), its framing camera's photographs and the DLR's models of their shapes made from them, as the USGS's planetary maps have them; the small craters and the rocks close by are made up.

          Vehicle models

            World Drive is checked by Cloudflare Turnstile so the free drives go to people rather than robots.

            How scoring works

            Get some air (a ramp, a crest, a hop on Space, or hit a wall to bounce off it), do tricks, and land on your wheels.
