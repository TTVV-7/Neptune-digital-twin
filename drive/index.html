          Drive anywhere on Earth in 3D

          World Drive is a free arcade driving game that runs in your browser, with nothing to download. Pick any place on the globe, from your own street to Times Square or a pass in the Alps, drop in and drive it in photorealistic 3D: the 3D world is Google's map of the real one, so the buildings, bridges, hills and roads are where they really are.

          What you can do

          • Drive anywhere. Search for a city, a landmark or an address, type coordinates, or tap the globe.
          • Stunts. Hit a wall and you bounce off it into the air: spin, flip and roll, land on your wheels to score, and chain tricks into combos.
          • Boost and drift. The booster takes you into overdrive, glowing pads on the road fill it up, and a drift pays out a mini-turbo.
          • Fly. Lift off over the rooftops and glide back down.
          • Drive on the Moon. Switch the globe to the Moon and drive its craters, seas and the Apollo landing sites, in low gravity, with the Earth in the sky.
          • Drive with others. See who's driving nearby as ghost cars, or invite friends to a party with a link, wherever each of you is.
          • Levels and the leaderboard. Driving earns XP for 50 levels of cars, paint, boost trails and titles, and your best runs go on the leaderboard.
          • Your ride. A pickup, a go-kart or a muscle car to start, and six more cars as you level up, from a golf cart and a tuk-tuk to a formula racer, all painted in the Garage; the optional Supporter Pass adds more.

          How to play

          W S gas and brake, A D steer, Space drift, Shift boost, F fly, R back on the road and M back to the globe. On a phone or a tablet, buttons on the screen.

          Questions

          Is it free?
          Yes. The 3D map costs World Drive a little for every visit, so there's a number of free drives each day, for everyone and for each visitor. The Supporter Pass, a one-time payment, adds rides and looks and more drives a day; you never need it to play.
          Does it work on a phone?
          Yes, in the browser on a phone, a tablet or a computer with 3D graphics (WebGL). Touch screens get driving buttons.
          Can I drive my own street?
          Yes: search for your address. Where Google's 3D map has no buildings modelled, you drive on the land itself.
          Can I drive on the Moon?
          Yes: switch the globe to the Moon at the top of this panel, or pick one of its places above. Its mountains, craters and seas are the real Moon's, from NASA's maps, and its gravity is a sixth of the Earth's, so a jump lasts two and a half times as long. On the Moon you drive on your own.
          Can I play with friends?
          Yes. Invite friends gives you a link; whoever opens it joins your party and can drive beside you. Cars are ghosts that pass through each other, and there's no chat.
          What does World Drive keep about me?
          Only what the leaderboard and levels need, and never where you really are: see About World Drive.

          World Drive

          Pick a spot anywhere on Earth, drop in and drive the real world in 3D: city streets, mountain passes, bridges, wherever you like. Or switch to the Moon and drive that.

          The 3D world is Google's photorealistic map, streamed as you go. Every visit costs a little, so there's a limit on free drives each day, for everyone and for each visitor. A visit lasts as long as you keep the page open: fly off and drop in somewhere else as often as you like.

          The leaderboard keeps the nickname you pick and your best scores, with where you set them and in what, and your XP and level, with the areas and start spots you've driven (until you pick a nickname, that's kept in this browser); nothing else about you.

          While Show me to drivers nearby is on (it is unless you switch it off in the pause menu), or while you're in a party, the others driving there see your leaderboard nickname (or a guest name) with your level and title, your ride and where your car is in the game, including the spot you dropped in and its name. Nothing else about you, and never where you really are.

          With it on, you're also counted on the globe's map of who's driving now, which everyone sees: a dot, to about a kilometre, where your car is in the game (or, while nobody else is driving in your area, where you dropped in, which can be a place you searched for), with how many are driving there. No name, no place name and nothing else about you. In a party, on the globe, or with it off, you're not counted.

          To see how the game is played, World Drive counts, for each day, things like how long people drive, on the Earth or the Moon and in which rides, which start spots they drop in at and how long that took, how far into the game a visit gets, whether it's on a phone, a tablet or a computer, how many searches are made (never what was typed), what they change in the Garage, what went wrong (a place with no 3D map, a ride that didn't load), which site they came from and how long the page took to load. Only totals, with nothing about who: no cookies, and nothing that tells one visitor from another.

          Signing in with Google is only for the Supporter Pass. It keeps your Google account's name and email address, whether you have the pass, and which nickname is yours, so the pass and its ⭐ follow you to any browser. Payments are made with Stripe: World Drive never sees your card.

          Map data © Google and its data providers. Place search © OpenStreetMap contributors.

          The Moon: its pictures and heights are NASA's, from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter's cameras (LROC) and laser altimeter (LOLA), as NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio's CGI Moon Kit has them; the small craters and the rocks close by are made up. While you're on the Moon you drive on your own: nobody sees your car, and you don't see theirs.
